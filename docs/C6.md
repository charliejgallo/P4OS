# The ESP32-C6

The P4 has no radio. The board's ESP32-C6 does the Wi-Fi (and can do BLE)
and talks to the P4 over SDIO, 4 bits at 40 MHz, through Espressif's
esp_hosted: the P4 runs its host side (`espressif/esp_hosted` in
`main/idf_component.yml`), the C6 its co-processor side. The P4 resets the C6
through its EN line (P4 GPIO54) at every boot.

## Versions

| | esp_hosted | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| P4 | 3.0.9 | since P4OS 0.7; 0.6 and before ran 1.4.7 |
| C6, factory | "0.0.0" | Waveshare's: esp-hosted `release/ng-v1.0.2-330`, built Oct 2024 on IDF 5.4-dev; it does not answer the version request |
| C6, `c6/` | 3.0.9 | Wi-Fi and BLE (HCI over SDIO) |

A 3.x host still talks to the factory C6 (Espressif's
`docs/migration.md`), so the P4 moved first and the C6 can follow when its
owner wants. The boot log says what the C6 runs:

    eh_init_evt: esp-hosted fw versions: host=3.0.9 coprocessor=0.0.0

and Settings, Update and Diagnostics show it.

## Building it

    tools/build_c6.sh

builds `c6/` (Espressif's `bleprph_wifi_coex/cp` example, pinned to the same
esp_hosted as the P4) into `c6/build/p4os_c6.bin`, about 1.4 MB: it fits a
slot of the factory partition table either way (1.5 or 1.75 MB).
The CI builds it too (the `c6` job of `.github/workflows/build.yml`), so a
change to `c6/` is checked on every push. A release carries `c6.bin`, for the
card's `/firmware`, and installs as below; 0.8.0 attached the image tested on
the board in 0.7.0 instead of the CI's, which is the same version built
elsewhere and had not been on a C6 yet.

## Installing it

From the P4, with no cable to the C6:

    tools/install_c6.sh p4os.local

uploads the image to the card's `/firmware/c6.bin` and starts the update.
Or copy the file there by hand (portal, Files) and use **Settings, Update,
"Install on the C6"**. Or `POST /api/c6/update`, with `GET /api/c6` for its
progress.

What happens:

1. The P4 checks the file: an app image, for the ESP32-C6 (the chip id in
   its header), with an app descriptor. Anything else is refused before a
   byte goes out.
2. It sends it with esp_hosted's co-processor OTA: begin, 1400-byte chunks,
   end. The C6 writes it into the slot it is not running and checks it
   (`esp_ota_end`): a cut transfer or a corrupt image leaves the running
   firmware where it was.
3. A C6 from esp_hosted 2.6 on switches slots on `activate`; older ones
   (the factory's) switch at the end and restart by themselves.
4. The P4 restarts, resets the C6, and both start over.

There is no Wi-Fi while it runs. On the board (2026-10-03) the 1.4 MB went
over in about 12 s, and the board was back on the network some 20 s later.
The C6 link watchdog (`docs/BUILDING.md`) stands aside meanwhile.

Tested both ways: factory to 3.0.9 (no `activate`), 3.0.9 to 3.0.9 and to
2.12.13 and back, and 3.0.9 back to the factory firmware (below).

## Throughput, and the C6's buffers

A C6 on esp_hosted 2.x or 3.x takes its Wi-Fi buffers from the P4 at
`esp_wifi_init`: the `CONFIG_WIFI_RMT_*` options in the P4's
`sdkconfig.defaults` are the C6's RAM, not the P4's. The factory firmware
ignored them. With 16 static TX buffers (esp_wifi_remote's default) a new
C6 could not fill an aggregation window and downloads from the board fell
to 0.4 MB/s; with 32 they are back. Measured with the portal (a 9.7 MB file
from the card, which itself reads at 14.6 MB/s, `GET /api/fs/bench`):

| C6 | Download | Upload |
|---|---|---|
| factory | 1.0-1.3 MB/s | 0.57-0.64 MB/s |
| 3.0.9, 16 TX buffers | 0.4-0.6 MB/s | 0.66-0.74 MB/s |
| 2.12.13, 16 TX buffers | 0.33-0.42 MB/s | 0.62 MB/s |
| 2.12.13, 32 TX buffers | 1.0-1.3 MB/s | - |
| 3.0.9, 32 TX buffers | 0.8-1.3 MB/s | 0.63 MB/s |

Not the cause, ruled out on the way: the access point being up, Wi-Fi power
save (the C6 confirms `WIFI_PS_NONE`), BLE coexistence (a C6 built without
BT was as slow), 802.11ax (the router here only does b/g/n), the SDIO mode
(streaming and SW_AGGR alike) and esp_hosted's buffers or stacks in PSRAM
(internal RAM gained ~10 % for 22 KB, not worth it). The ping wanders
between 5 and 100 ms with every firmware, the factory one too: that is the
radio here, not the C6.

## What can go wrong

The C6 has no rollback: a firmware that is sound but cannot talk to the P4
(a wrong transport, another esp_hosted major) stays, and the board has no
network. The P4 can then do nothing more for it, and the way back is the
C6's own serial port.

## Recovery: the C6's serial port (J7)

The board has a 4-pin connector, J7, with the C6's GND, IO9 (its BOOT
strap), U0RXD and U0TXD (Waveshare's schematic; their FAQ has the
procedure). The C6's EN is not on it: it is the P4's GPIO54, with a pull-up.

You need a USB-serial adapter at **3.3 V**. On this board J7 has bare holes;
test clips on them did the job. They did not hold 460800 baud: 115200 is
the speed for them (a 4 MB read takes about 6 minutes).

A failed command at a speed the clips cannot carry leaves esptool's stub on
the C6 listening at that speed. Unplugging the board alone does not reset
it, as the adapter keeps the C6 powered through its TX line: unplug both,
then start again.

1. Adapter GND to J7 GND, adapter RX to C6_U0TXD, adapter TX to C6_U0RXD.
2. Hold the C6's IO9 to GND (a jumper on J7).
3. Unplug the board, hold the P4's BOOT and plug it back in: both start in
   download mode, the P4 because of BOOT (and it leaves GPIO54 alone, so the
   pull-up lets the C6 run), the C6 because of IO9. The screen stays dark.
4. From `c6/`:

       esptool.py --chip esp32c6 -p /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX -b 115200 \
         --before no_reset --after no_reset \
         write_flash 0x0 build/bootloader/bootloader.bin \
         0x8000 build/partition_table/partition-table.bin \
         0xd000 build/ota_data_initial.bin 0x10000 build/p4os_c6.bin

5. Take the jumper off IO9 and power the board off and on.

## The backup

Before the first update, keep the C6's flash as it came. With the C6 in
download mode as above:

    esptool.py --chip esp32c6 -p /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX -b 115200 \
      --before no_reset --after no_reset read_flash 0 0x400000 c6/backup/c6-factory.bin

(`flash_id` says its size; this board's C6 is an ESP32-C6FH4, 4 MB inside.)
`write_flash 0 c6-factory.bin` puts it all back. `c6/backup/` stays out of
git: the image is Waveshare's, and its NVS may hold Wi-Fi credentials.

## Going back to the factory firmware, with no cable

The backup holds the factory app, and the P4 can send it like any other
image:

    tools/c6_app_from_flash.py c6/backup/c6-factory.bin
    tools/install_c6.sh p4os.local c6/backup/c6-factory-app.bin

Done on the board: the C6 came back as "0.0.0" and the network with it.
